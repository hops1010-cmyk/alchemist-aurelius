## Book Twenty-Six: The King Asks

### Chapter 151: Please Help Us

"Please help us, Aurelius," the king said. He said it in the courtyard, where the wretches had been, where the starving had been, where the king had said "Get those wretches out of the courtyard. Guard! GUARD!" The courtyard was empty now. The wretches were gone. The starving were gone. The guards were gone. The system was down, and the courtyard was empty, and the king was standing in it alone, and the alone was the silence, and the silence was the system, and the system was down.

"Please help us, Aurelius," the king said again. He said it to Aurelius, who was standing in the courtyard, who had come back from Barcelona, who had packed his bag and his tools and his mortar and his tinctures and his salves and his poultices and his quarks and his map and his code and his oscillator, and who had walked through the gates of the castle, and the gates were open, and the gates were open because the system was down, and the system was down because the quarks had stopped connecting.

Aurelius looked at the king. He looked at the courtyard. He looked at the empty space where the wretches had been, where the starving had been, where the king had said "Get those wretches out of the courtyard."

"You said 'Get those wretches out of the courtyard,'" Aurelius said. "You said it to the guards. You said it because you did not care. You said it because the system was working, and the system was working because I was building it, and I was building it because you took me, and you took me because you wanted castles, and you wanted castles because you were the king, and the king was the Ruler, and the Ruler was the archetype, and the archetype was the symbol, and the symbol was the quark, and the quark was the network, and the network was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was down."

"I know," the king said. "I know. I did not care. I should have cared. I am sorry. I am asking. Please help us."

Aurelius looked at the king. He looked at the courtyard. He looked at the empty space.

"I will help you," Aurelius said. "Not for you. For them. For the wretches. For the starving. For the people. For the system. For the wholeness. For the point."

He opened his bag. He took out his tools. He took out his mortar. He took out his tinctures and his salves and his poultices. He took out his quarks and his map and his code and his oscillator.

He began to build.

### Chapter 152: The Rebuilding

Aurelius rebuilt the system. He rebuilt it the way he had built it the first time — one stone at a time, one quark at a time, one connection at a time. He rebuilt it with the mortar he had mixed, and the geometry he had learned, and the knowledge he had carried from Barcelona, from the East, from the West, from the castle, from the tunnel, from the escape.

He rebuilt the quarks. He rebuilt the connections. He rebuilt the oscillator. He rebuilt the time frequency. He rebuilt the 2.5-second gap. He rebuilt the memory. He rebuilt the wholeness.

The system came back. It came back slowly, like a light turning on, like a sound returning, like a breath being taken after a long silence. The quarks connected. The ports responded. The agents worked. The commands ran. The map updated. The bento grid lit up. The headless browser opened. The reverse proxy hummed. The tunnel formed. The gateway unlocked.

The system came back, and the kingdom came back, and the people came back, and the wretches came back, and the starving came back, and the guards came back, and the courtyard was not empty anymore.

The king watched. He watched from the courtyard, and the watching was the seeing, and the seeing was the truth, and the truth was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

"Thank you, Aurelius," the king said.

"You're welcome," Aurelius said. "But this is the last time. The last time I build it. The last time I save it. The last time I come back. From now on, the system is yours. The Tower is yours. The wholeness is yours. Build it right. Build it strong. Build it to last."

He turned. He walked out of the courtyard. He walked through the gates. He walked into the world, and the world was the world, and the system was whole, and the wholeness was the point, and the point was the beginning, and the beginning was always.
