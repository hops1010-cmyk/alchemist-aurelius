## Start Again (600 words)
Aurelius stood at the edge of the village at dawn, chalk in hand, the morning air smelling of lime and iron. The king's carriage had not yet arrived, but it would. It always did. He set the chalk down and looked at the barn — his father's barn, rebuilt three times, each rebuild a lesson. The storm had taught him drainage. The fire had taught him ventilation. The knock-out wall had taught him load paths, and how a wall that looks solid can be hollow at its heart.

He thought of Furball, asleep in the mortar trough, purring like a small engine. He thought of Elizabeth and Anne, the twins who had been arguing since before they were born. He thought of James, quiet and watchful, and Henry, all noise and motion. He thought of William with his books, and Charles with his blocks, and Archibald, the baby who laughed at everything.

The king would come. The king would take him. The king would want castles, and Aurelius would build them, because building was the gift and the gift was the burden and the burden was the journey. But first, there was this morning. This chalk. This barn. This life.

He picked up the chalk. He drew a line on the floor. The line was straight. The line was true. The line was the beginning.

The king came at noon, with six guards and a carriage and a look that said he had been thinking for a long time. Aurelius did not bow. The king did not mind. "You are the one who builds strong castles," the king said. It was not a question.

"I am the one who builds castles that stand," Aurelius said. "Strong is a word other people use."

The king looked at the chalk lines, the models, the mortar samples, the oven, the bellows, the limestone. He looked at all of it, and then he looked at Aurelius, and his face did something complicated.

"You will come with me," the king said. "You will build for me. You will build castles that stand, and you will not stop until I tell you to stop."

"And my family?" Aurelius asked.

The king did not answer. The morning suddenly felt smaller than it had a moment before. Furball purred in the mortar trough, unaware that everything was about to change. And Aurelius, the alchemist, the builder, the stone man, the castle man, looked at the chalk line on the floor — straight, true, the beginning — and knew that the beginning was always.
