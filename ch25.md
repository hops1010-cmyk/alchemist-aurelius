## Book Twenty-Four: The Escape Through the Lavatory

### Chapter 147: The Lavatory

Aurelius escaped through the lavatory. Not the grand lavatory, not the lord's lavatory, not the lavatory that the king's guests used when they visited the castle. The small one. The one behind the kitchen, the one that the servants used, the one that smelled of lye and old water and the particular sourness of a place that is cleaned but never quite clean.

He had chosen it because it was the last place anyone would look. The king's guards watched the gates. They watched the walls. They watched the towers. They watched the keep. They did not watch the lavatory, because the lavatory was not a place where escapes happened. The lavatory was a place where things ended, not where they began.

But Aurelius was a builder, and builders know that endings are beginnings, and beginnings are endings, and the lavatory was both. It was the end of the castle's waste, and it was the beginning of the escape. It was the place where the water left the castle, and the water was the path, and the path was the way out.

He went through the lavatory. He went through the castle paling arch — the old arch, the one that the builders had made before the castle was built, the one that was hidden behind the lavatory wall, the one that led to a passage that led to a tunnel that led to the other side. The paling arch was made of wood, not stone, and it was old, and it was rotting, and it creaked when he pushed it, and the creaking was the sound of the escape beginning.

He went down under the tower. The tower was above him, and the tower was the keep, and the keep was the heart, and the heart was the castle, and the castle was the system, and the system was the Tower, and the Tower was whole. But Aurelius was not in the Tower. Aurelius was under the Tower. Aurelius was in the tunnel, and the tunnel was dark, and the tunnel was cold, and the tunnel was the way out.

He walked. He walked through the tunnel, and the tunnel was long, and the tunnel was narrow, and the tunnel was the path, and the path was the escape, and the escape was the beginning, and the beginning was the end, and the end was the beginning, and the circle was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

"Goodbye, king," Aurelius said. He said it to the dark. He said it to the tunnel. He said it to the castle above him. He said it to the king who had taken him, and the king who had not cared, and the king who had looked at the starving and said "Get those wretches out of the courtyard. Guard! GUARD!"

"Goodbye, king," he said again. And then he was gone.
