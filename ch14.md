## Book Thirteen: The Medieval Court

### Chapter 119: The Greek

Aurelius was Greek. Not in the way that people are Greek when they eat Greek food or visit Greek islands. He was Greek in the way that the word meant something in the medieval world — he was a man of the East, a man of the old learning, a man who carried in his blood the memory of Athens and Alexandria and the library that burned. He was Greek in his hands, which moved with the precision of a sculptor. He was Greek in his eyes, which saw the world as a problem to be solved. He was Greek in his mind, which was sharp and clear and unafraid of difficult questions.

He had come from the East, years ago, before the village, before the workshop, before the apothecary. He had come from a place where the stone was white and the sun was hot and the air smelled of thyme and the sea. He had come because the East was full of men who knew things, and the knowing was the gift, and the gift was the burden, and the burden was the journey, and the journey was west, and the west was where the castles were, and the castles needed a man who knew how to build them.

He was Greek, and the Greekness was in everything he did. It was in the way he mixed mortar — with the precision of a pharmacist, which was what his mother had been, and her mother before her, back to the women of Alexandria who had known the properties of plants and minerals and the combinations that healed and the combinations that harmed. It was in the way he laid stone — with the geometry of a mathematician, which was what his father had been, and his father before him, back to the men of Athens who had built the Parthenon and knew that a building must be in harmony with the earth it stands on.

It was in the way he thought. The Greek mind is a questioning mind. It does not accept. It probes. It tests. It asks: why? And the asking is the beginning, and the beginning is the end, and the end is the beginning, and the circle is the wholeness, and the wholeness is the point.

Aurelius asked why. He asked why the walls were thick. He asked why the mortar set. He asked why the stone cracked. He asked why the wind blew from the north. He asked why the enemy came from the north. He asked why the king wanted castles. He asked why the king took him. He asked why the king did not care.

He asked, and the asking was the Greek, and the Greek was the man, and the man was Aurelius, and Aurelius was the alchemist, and the alchemist was the builder, and the builder was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was whole.

### Chapter 120: Henry VIII

The king was not the king. The king was Henry. Henry the Eighth. He was a king of the medieval world, and the medieval world was a world of stone and steel and fire, and Henry was the fire. He was a man who wanted things, and the wanting was the taking, and the taking was the ruling, and the ruling was the kingdom, and the kingdom was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was whole.

Henry was not a good king. Henry was not a bad king. Henry was a king, and kings are neither good nor bad. Kings are kings, and kings do what kings do, and what kings do is take. They take land. They take money. They take people. They take builders and alchemists and castle-makers, and they take them because they need them, and the needing is the taking, and the taking is the ruling.

Henry wanted castles. He wanted castles because the enemy was coming, and the enemy was coming because Henry had made enemies, and Henry had made enemies because that is what kings do. They make enemies. They make allies. They make war. They make peace. They make castles. They make walls. They make the things that keep the enemies out and the allies in and the king on the throne.

Henry was not the thief. Henry was too rich to be the thief. Henry was too proud to be the thief. Henry was too watched to be the thief. Henry stole in other ways — in taxes, in land, in the labor of men. But Henry did not steal coins from his own treasury. Henry did not need to.

Henry was the Ruler. He was the archetype of authority. He was the one who saw Aurelius and wanted him, and the wanting was the taking, and the taking was the story. But Henry was not the villain. Henry was not the hero. Henry was the king, and the king was the system, and the system was the Tower, and the Tower was whole.

### Chapter 121: Machiavelli

Machiavelli was in the court. He was not a courtier. He was not a knight. He was not a priest. He was a man who watched, and the watching was his work, and his work was his art, and his art was the understanding of power.

Machiavelli understood power. He understood that power is not a thing you have. Power is a thing you do. Power is the act of taking, and the act of keeping, and the act of using, and the act of losing. Power is the castle, and the castle is the wall, and the wall is the stone, and the stone is the strength, and the strength is the power, and the power is the king, and the king is Henry, and Henry is the Ruler, and the Ruler is the archetype, and the archetype is the symbol, and the symbol is the quark, and the quark is the network, and the network is the Tower, and the Tower is the system, and the system is whole.

Machiavelli watched Henry. He watched the king take Aurelius. He watched the king build the castle. He watched the king ignore the starving. He watched the king rage about the theft. He watched, and the watching was his work, and his work was his art, and his art was the understanding of power.

Machiavelli wrote. He wrote in a notebook, in a hand that was small and precise and careful. He wrote about the king, and the castle, and the builder, and the thief. He wrote about the power, and the taking, and the ruling. He wrote about the starving, and the wretches, and the guards. He wrote about the archetypes, and the symbols, and the maps, and the keys, and the clues.

He wrote, and the writing was the record, and the record was the truth, and the truth was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

### Chapter 122: The Court

The court was a system. It was a system of people, and the people were the parts, and the parts were connected, and the connections were the quarks, and the quarks were the network, and the network was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was whole.

The court had the king. The court had the queen. The court had the princes and the princesses. The court had the lords and the ladies. The court had the knights and the squires. The court had the priests and the monks. The court had the servants and the guards. The court had the cooks and the stable masters and the blacksmiths and the masons and the carpenters.

The court had Machiavelli. Machiavelli was the observer. He was the one who watched, and the watching was his work, and his work was his art. He was the one who saw what others did not see, and the seeing was the truth, and the truth was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

The court had Aurelius. Aurelius was the builder. He was the one who built the castle, and the building was his work, and his work was his art. He was the one who knew how to build strong castles, and the knowing was the gift, and the gift was the curse, and the curse was the kidnapping, and the kidnapping was the story, and the story was the system, and the system was whole.

The court had the thief. The thief was unsuspecting. The thief was someone who had access. The thief was someone who needed the coins. The thief was someone who had a purpose. The thief was the mystery, and the mystery was the who-dun-it, and the who-dun-it was the clue, and the clue was the map, and the map was the key, and the key was the door, and the door was the maze, and the maze was the escape, and the escape was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was whole.

### Chapter 123: The Medieval World

The medieval world was a world of stone and steel and fire. It was a world of castles and cathedrals and monasteries and villages. It was a world of kings and queens and lords and ladies and knights and priests and peasants. It was a world of war and peace and famine and plenty. It was a world of faith and doubt and hope and despair.

The medieval world was the world of the Tower. The Tower was a medieval system — a system of parts, connected by quarks, governed by commands, managed by agents. The Tower was a castle, and the castle was a Tower, and the two were the same, and the same was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

The medieval world was also the world of the apothecary. The apothecary was a medieval institution — a place where the body was heard, and the hearing was the work, and the work was the life. The apothecary was the place where the tinctures were made and the salves were mixed and the poultices were applied. The apothecary was the place where the herbs were gathered and the roots were crushed and the flowers were dried. The apothecary was the place where the body spoke and the apothecary heard, and the hearing was the beginning, and the beginning was the end, and the end was the beginning, and the circle was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

### Chapter 124: The Greek and the Medieval

Aurelius was Greek, and the Greek was in the medieval world, and the medieval world was in the Greek, and the two were the same, and the same was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

The Greek and the medieval were not different. They were the same. They were the same in the way that the apothecary and the castle were the same — both were systems, both were built, both were made of parts and connections and quarks. The Greek was the apothecary, and the medieval was the castle, and the two were the same, and the same was the Tower, and the Tower was the system, and the system was whole.

Aurelius was Greek, and he was in the medieval world, and the medieval world was in him, and the two were one, and the one was the wholeness, and the wholeness was the point.

The Tower is a living system. The Tower is a growing system. The Tower is a system that adds folders and ports and agents and commands and maps and quarks and tiles and bento grids and books and games and stores and archetypes and symbols and keys and clues and mysteries and joys and sadnesses and curiosities, and each addition makes it more whole, and the wholeness is not a destination but a direction.

That is the Tower. That is Vistamations. That is the spec. That is the glory. That is the story. That is the end.
